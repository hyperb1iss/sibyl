Sibyl 1.5Trust It

1.5 makes accumulated agent experience dependable in daily use. Project boundaries hold, corrections and deletions stick, recall stays fast on a large graph, and the dream cycle learns from the right evidence.

The learning claim rides along only if a sealed study earns it. A study that comes back inconclusive changes the release note, not the release date.

At a glance

Workstream 1Boundaries and lifecycle that hold~$10
Workstream 2A learning loop worth measuring, then one credible study≤ $425
Workstream 3Fast on a big graph, under load$0 incremental
Workstream 4One real team move$0 incremental

Four workstreams in priority order, with a hard ceiling of $435 in inference spend and a likely spend nearer $300. Sibyl is an open-source project, so the plan buys evidence as cheaply as the statistics allow. The scale and migration work runs on the devbox and free public-repo CI, and engineering time is not counted. The release ships when the trust, scale, spend-guard and migration-rehearsal gates pass. The study runs alongside and decides only what the release note may claim. Every gate figure in this document is a proposed acceptance criterion, not a measurement, and every spend figure is a ceiling awaiting approval.

01

Decision

1.5 is a trust release. 1.4 proved that the dream cycle runs end to end with receipts, and the September 25 cleanup fixed the reasons it learned the wrong things. What a user meets first, though, is not consolidation. They meet recall that crosses projects, a delete that returns 500, a correction they can't undo from the CLI, and a graph page that takes a minute and then fails. 1.5 fixes those, makes the system hold up at the size real graphs reach, and delivers one real team migration.

The learning proof stays a first-class workstream, but it doesn't gate the release. The study is staged behind cheap gates, so money is spent only once the pipeline can plausibly show an effect, and the whole plan's inference spend is capped at $435. If the sealed study passes the 1.4 claim gate, 1.5 carries a bounded claim. If it doesn't, the release note reports the measured result and 1.5 still ships.

1.5 does not: activate Jev in production, claim general or autonomous learning, chase leaderboard numbers, polish graph visuals beyond the capacity fix, expand the archive or memoryfield surfaces, or build team workflow features beyond migration. Proposals in those areas go on the 1.6 list.

02

Where 1.4 left us

The learning mechanism now keeps what matters

The September 20 re-probe showed memory of any kind lifting the three headroom tasks, but native memory did not beat raw retrieval (native 12/15, raw 13/15). The cause was consolidation: 33 of the 34 procedures it promoted were generic repair choreography. Four PRs merged on September 25 address that.

The intervention probe (task b7bc8bf4) gives the only native-over-raw signal so far: 14/18 against 9/20 on cdn-batch-map, a paired +6 of 18 at p = 0.11. That clears the pre-stated +4 threshold but isn't significant. On the 16 repetitions where both arms finished, removing the derived items cost only 2 of those wins (11/18 overall against 14/18), a family summary adds nothing over raw, and a task with opaque keys shows no negative transfer (10/10 in both arms). One more finding from the probe: the promoted pattern itself was excluded from native recall as out of scope, and the mechanism reached the solver only through two raw candidate rows (task 3ed9000e).

What a user meets first

The September 22 demo prep and the proof work surfaced trust defects, and every one of them is still present on main. Each is small and reproduced. The gap that matters most is in process: PR CI never runs the :check bundle, so a delegated-scope regression from PR 525 shipped through 11 days of green PRs and surfaced only at the 1.4.0 dry cut.

The system slows as it grows

On the real 10.5K-entity graph, /graph spins for about 70 seconds and then fails with a 500, starving other pages in the same tab. A long-lived Surreal process reached 33 GB resident, with plain selects taking 14.8 seconds. Sustained ingest degrades from about 10 seconds to 100 to 700 seconds per capture once the entity table deepens. These also contaminate every latency number the public benchmarks would report.

Public benchmarks have no current anchor

LongMemEval-S sits at 26.2% because the adapter truncates every session at 3,985 characters and never calls compile_context. V2 Web Small's 23.3% ran with notes off and isn't comparable to the notes-on 30.38% anchor. The 80% and 60% targets in the 1.4 plan were never measured.

Spend so far

The 1.4 campaign spent about $463: roughly $385 on memory calls and $8.50 on solver attempts through September 19, then $69.47 across the headroom screen, re-probe, three scoped cycles and the intervention.

03

Release rules

  1. Trust gates run on every PR. Nothing that affects scope, authorization or lifecycle merges without the gate bundle having run on its head.
  2. Scale fixes grow capacity. Truncating results, lowering concurrency or skipping work doesn't count as a fix. A temporary mitigation needs a name, a tracking task and a stated path back.
  3. Every paid run sits behind a gate and a guard. Each spend stage unlocks only when the previous gate passes. No product default ships with unbounded model spend.
  4. Claims need sealed evidence. Design, analysis and exclusions are frozen before unsealing. Failures stay in the denominators, and nothing is rerun selectively.
  5. Verification is independent. Every non-trivial PR gets an adversarial verifier that did not write it, plus root moon run :lint and :typecheck before push.

Task families with headroom

Only cdn-batch-map has real native-versus-raw room today; stock-adjustment-log and department-unit-budget saturate near raw. The study needs harder families before it needs a bigger n. Keep cdn-batch-map and add two families with different mechanisms, for example retry with idempotency keys, and revision-sensitive authorization. Each family needs:

  • At least 25 instances: 5 for development screening and 20 sealed.
  • Cases where applying the remembered procedure would be wrong, so negative transfer is measurable.
  • Task validity verified independently of any memory arm, with public checks the solver can run and hidden checks it can't.
  • A learning corpus of episodes that captures the family's mechanism, generated the way screen48's was.

The saturated screen48 tasks become regression controls.

Staged spend

$30Headroom screen5 development instances per family, 3 reps, 3 arms (135 attempts). Unlocks when raw succeeds 30 to 70% and the best simple control stays under 80%.
$20CanaryScoped cycle plus 10 paired reps on cdn-batch-map. Unlocks when promoted mechanisms reach recall, superseded drafts don't, and exceptions survive.
$75Fresh cycleOne ordinary dream cycle over the study families' learning episodes, with the existing screen48 memory as distractors that are already paid for. Unlocks when the canary passes.
$300Sealed study20 unseen instances per family × 5 arms, with repetitions set by the power preflight (default 4), about 1,200 attempts plus 100 on contrast families. One interim look at the halfway point stops it for futility.

The study is sized to what the statistics need. Uncertainty is clustered by instance, so once each instance has a few repetitions, more repetitions add little power; new instances add a lot. The plan keeps 20 sealed instances per family and lets the screen's variance set the repetitions. At the intervention's measured $0.19 per attempt ($29.98 for 160 cells), 1,300 attempts cost about $250, and the $300 cap leaves room for harder tasks running longer. The interim look means about half the time the study stops at half the cost.

Evaluation contract

ElementContract
Armsnative (product recall after the fresh cycle), raw (raw retrieval over the same corpus), strong summary (the best simple control), no memory, and native minus derived (native with the promoted items removed and no refill).
UnitOne attempt at a fixed task budget. The primary endpoint is success on hidden checks.
PairingEach instance and repetition shares one seed across all arms (the per-repetition seeds from #657).
Freeze before unsealingSolver model and budget, memory model, corpus and checkpoint, seeds, exclusion rules, and the analysis script with its hash.
AnalysisPaired differences with uncertainty clustered by instance (cluster bootstrap), reported per family and pooled. A negative family is reported, never averaged away.
Claim gate (from 1.4)At least +10 points over no memory and at least +5 over the best simple control, with positive paired lower 95% bounds.
Also requiredA positive native-minus-derived ablation, negative-transfer bounds on the exception cases, repeated-mistake rates, and cost per success with consolidation cost amortized in.
Power preflightFrom the screen's variance, pick the repetitions per instance (2 to 6), favouring instances over repetitions. The sealed design must have at least 80% power to detect +5 over the best simple control within the $300 cap. If it doesn't, no claim-bearing run happens and the gate stays where it is.
Interim lookOne pre-registered analysis at half the attempts, with alpha spending (O'Brien-Fleming boundaries). It can stop for futility, or for success only if the boundary is crossed. The final analysis uses the adjusted threshold.
MultiplicityThe primary endpoint is the pooled paired difference across families, clustered by instance. cdn-batch-map is pre-registered as the primary family. Per-family claims are secondary: a family may be named in the note only if the pooled gate passes and its own lower bound stays positive after Holm adjustment across the families tested. Nominal per-family bounds are reported but never carry a claim on their own.
IntegrityVoided cells (provider errors, harness faults) are rerun under the same seeds and listed, and failures stay in the denominators. Nothing is rerun for any other reason.

Risk register

Biggest risk to the thesis

Consolidation may add little beyond better raw retrieval. The intervention already hints at it: on the 16 paired repetitions, removing the promoted items cost only 2 of native's 6 extra wins. The earliest cheap signal is the $20 canary after 3ed9000e and f1093a5a. If the promoted mechanism now reaches the solver but removing it still changes nothing, pause the study and investigate consumption. That result stops the spend; it doesn't prove zero benefit.
